Burger Recipe

  1. Combine: Mix 1 lb of ground beef, 1 large egg, and seasonings (salt, pepper, garlic powder) in a bowl. Avoid overmixing, which can make the meat tough.
  2. Form: Gently shape the mixture into four patties, about 1 inch thicker and wider than your bun, as they will shrink slightly while cooking.
  3. ndent: Press a small dimple in the center of each patty with your thumb to prevent it from puffing up.
  4. Chill: Refrigerate the patties for at least 30 minutes to help them hold their shape when cooked. Cooking and assembly
  5. Cook: Heat a skillet or grill to medium-high heat. Cook the patties for 4–5 minutes per side, or to your desired doneness.

  • War-time rebranding: During World War I, hamburgers were called "liberty sandwiches" to stir up patriotism and disassociate them from Germany.

History Of Burger

By the mid-19th century, German immigrants brought a dish called the "Hamburg steak" to America, which was a fried, flattened meatball or patty of chopped beef served on a plate. This dish gained popularity in the United States before the invention of the sandwich.Its history traces back to German "Hamburg steaks" which were ground beef patties served on a plate. The modern hamburger, a ground beef patty placed between two slices of bread or a bun, likely emerged as a portable street food at late 19th-century fairs and events. The standardization and popularization of the hamburger were significantly advanced by the White Castle fast-food chain in 1921, which marketed the hygienic and affordable burger to the masses.
