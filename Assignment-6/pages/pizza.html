Pizza

Pizza Recipes

  1. Combine 1 cup warm water, 2 1/4 tsp active dry yeast, and 1 tbsp sugar. Let it sit for 5 minutes until it becomes foamy.
  2. Add 2 1/2 cups all-purpose flour, 1 tsp salt, and 1 1/2 tbsp olive oil. Knead the dough until it's smooth and elastic.
  3. Grease a bowl with oil, place the dough in it, and cover. Let it rise for about 45 minutes to 1 hour, or until it has doubled in size.
  4. Assembly and baking Preheat your oven to 475°F (246°C). For a crispier crust, place a pizza stone or baking sheet in the oven while it preheats.
  5. Punch down the risen dough and divide it in half. On a floured surface, stretch or roll each half into a round pizza base.
  6. Brush the base with olive oil, then spread about 1/2 cup of pizza sauce over it. Top with shredded mozzarella cheese and your favorite toppings.
  7. Slide the pizza onto the preheated stone or baking sheet. Bake for 10–15 minutes, or until the cheese is bubbly and the crust is golden brown.

Fun Facts About Pizza

  • Out of this world: In 2001, a pizza was delivered to the International Space Station, making it the first pizza to be eaten in space.
  • A popular American food: October is National Pizza Month in the United States, and Americans consume billions of pizzas each year.
  • Pepperoni is a U.S. invention: Pepperoni is the most popular pizza topping in the U.S. but is not an Italian invention.
  • Ancient origins: While modern pizza originated in Naples, Italy, the concept of flatbreads with toppings has ancient roots, with early versions enjoyed by ancient civilizations like the Greeks and Romans

History of Pizza

Pizza's ancestors are the flatbreads eaten by ancient cultures like the Greeks and Romans. Ancient Greeks ate flatbreads like plausuntos, and Persian soldiers baked flatbread with cheese and dates on their shields. The first known mention of the word "pizza" was in a 997 CE Latin document from Gaeta, Italy, referring to a payment of "twelve pizzas".
